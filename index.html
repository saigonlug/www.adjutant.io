Case 01 / Lending software for banks

Built to onboard banks.
Missing the guardrails.

The business wanted to onboard banks quickly, each with different org charts, processes and policies. Product requirements stayed vague. The contracted tech team treated PostgreSQL like an unconstrained document store. No validation. No data constraints. Anyone—or any process—could write anything.

ISO 27001, SOC 2, GDPR. Compliance is the easier part. Preventing massive banking data corruption or a sweeping data leak that could end the business is the hard part.

↳

A structured foundation.
A migration out of the mess.

The work behind it +

This was a software company building a lending platform for banks. “Flexible” needed to mean configurable structures, workflows and policies. The product team left that undefined, and the tech team removed the rules from the data instead. Vague requirements and engineering mistakes happen as products grow. Without safeguards, they compound into risks the business may not see until much later.

One customer had “Married.” Another had no marital-status field. Another had an empty string, a space, or a value in another language. Staff were left guessing from spouse co-applicants or chasing Excel files. Basic customer facts had become detective work—on a platform moving billions of US dollars.

A poisoned record can feed the wrong lending decision. Uncontrolled access can expose sensitive data across customers or institutions. Compliance work cannot replace the engineering that prevents those failures. At banking scale, the consequences can threaten the entire business.

Our consultant helped untangle the records, define a structured model, and migrate the legacy data. Real flexibility needed a sound foundation: explicit identities, validated values and rules the database could enforce.

Case 02 / Financial platform

New client.
Another entire platform.

Paper filing procedures became database architecture. Identifiers and workflows tangled together. Digital work inherited the friction of the filing cabinet.

↳

A reusable core.
Room for each client’s workflow.

The work behind it +

Documents moved between officers for processing, compliance, and audit. The software reproduced the physical numbering and handoffs so literally that each institution required its own variation of the platform.

Our consultants helped separate document identity from workflow and rebuild the core to support different institutional needs. The work addressed tightly coupled data and processing rules that made the business difficult to scale.

Case 03 / Logistics platform

A 96xlarge database.
Still down every
two weeks.

A fast-growing logistics platform ran its entire backend and heavy analytics against one gigantic PostgreSQL database on an AWS 96xlarge instance. Major outages hit every two weeks. They had scaled the machine. The architectural failure was still there.

↳

Operations and analytics, decoupled.
Insight without the collision.

The work behind it +

A load balancer spread requests across copies of the same backend monolith. Every copy hammered the same master database. Reporting and analytics hammered it too. More application servers only sent more work to the same bottleneck.

Even a 96xlarge database could not fix that design. Production and analytics competed for the same resources, and the single point of failure took the platform down every two weeks. A bigger box was an expensive way to keep the same problem.

Our consultant helped move the platform toward a service-oriented architecture, separating data and workloads. Real-time analytics moved to an independent platform so decision-making no longer needed to compete with the operational workload.
